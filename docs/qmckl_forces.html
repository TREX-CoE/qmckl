Forces

1. Introduction

A force on a nucleus is minus the gradient of the energy with respect to the position of that nucleus. This module computes the derivatives of the pieces of the wavefunction that depend on the nuclei, not the forces themselves: the derivatives of the Jastrow factor and of the orbitals (values, gradients and Laplacians). They are stored in the context and read back with the qmckl_get_forces_... accessors; the caller combines them into the derivative of the local energy.

Moving one electron changes the Jastrow and therefore its derivative with respect to the nuclei. The single-electron sections compute the difference between the old and the new contribution, taking the new position from qmckl_set_single_point.

2. Forces of the Jastrow function

Only the electron-nucleus and electron-electron-nucleus Jastrow terms depend on the nuclear coordinates; the electron-electron Jastrow does not, so its derivative vanishes.

3. Forces of the orbitals

The molecular orbitals are linear combinations of the atomic orbitals, so the derivative of an MO quantity with respect to a nuclear coordinate is a contraction of the transposed MO coefficients with the derivative of the corresponding AO quantity, restricted to the AOs centered on that nucleus. The routines below compute those derivatives for the AO values, and for the MO values, gradients and Laplacians.

3.1. Force of AO value

Computes the derivative of the AO values with respect to the nuclear coordinates. For each nucleus, the entries of the AOs centered on it are set to minus their gradient, since moving the nucleus by δ is moving the electron by −δ.

Calculates the derivative of the MO values with respect to the nuclear coordinates, by contracting the transposed MO coefficients with the gradients of the AOs centered on that nucleus.
